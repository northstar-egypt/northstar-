Desktop. Comparison is not offered on phones. Three columns of numbers do not survive a 375 pixel screen, and pretending otherwise produces a worse tool than sending the user to a profile.

Annotations

  1. The age and maturity toggle is the screen. Comparing two 14 year olds where one is 18 months behind in maturity is comparing a child to an adolescent. Every academy that cuts players in the wrong month does it because they only ever saw the first view. Putting the second view one tap away is the product's argument, made operable.
  2. The banner explains what the current view distorts. Not a tooltip, not a footnote. A scout who never reads it still gets the sentence in their eyeline before they read the table.
  3. Two players minimum, three maximum. Four columns of numbers stop being a comparison and start being a spreadsheet, and the point of this screen is a judgement, not a data dump.
  4. Emphasis marks the higher value, it does not declare a winner. Higher is not better for sprint time, and the interface has to know that per metric rather than assuming bigger wins.
  5. Where the error bars overlap, the interface says so instead of ranking. This is the single most important honesty rule on the screen. Two forecasts that differ by less than their uncertainty are not different, and drawing one as better would be the exact failure mode this whole product is supposed to fix.

Data and API

  • Two or three Player records with their full Measurement series and PerformanceEntry rows.
  • Maturity offset and predicted adult height with error come from the ML layer. Without them the maturity toggle cannot exist, so this screen is partly blocked on that track.
  • Percentiles must be computed against a stated population and the same population for every player in the comparison, otherwise the columns are not comparable at all.
  • Every player in the comparison passes the same consent and role checks as the profile screen. Comparison must not become a way to read a record that the profile page would have withheld.
  • Metric direction, whether higher or lower is better, has to come from metric definitions rather than being hard coded in the frontend. That belongs in packages/shared.

Open questions

  • Can you compare across sports? Almost certainly not, and the interface should refuse rather than render a table of nulls.
  • Can you compare across tiers, a youth player against a professional? Interesting for a scout, misleading without heavy caveats.
  • Maturity offset needs a defined method. Skeletal age is not available, so it will be estimated from growth velocity and parental data we do not collect. The ML track owns this and the label on the row depends on what they choose.
  • Should a comparison be shareable as a link? Useful for a scouting report, and a quiet way for a minor's data to travel outside the platform.
